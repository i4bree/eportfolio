<div class="research-section">

## Research Focus

This project compares carbohydrate-active enzyme (CAZyme) annotations generated by two bioinformatic tools, dbCAN and Cayman, to characterize the ECM-degradation potential of the _B. fragilis_ pangenome and a panel of _Bacteroides_ type strains.

CAZymes are essential for degrading host- and diet-derived glycans, including components of the extracellular matrix (ECM). However, bioinformatic tools can vary in how thoroughly they predict substrate specificity for these enzymes, particularly for host-derived glycans.

By comparing these two tools, this project aims to clarify how tool choice affects the inferred ECM-degrading capacity of the _B. fragilis_ pangenome and strengthen the interpretation of CAZyme predictions in future studies of the gut microbiome.

</div>

<div class="research-section">

## My Responsibilities

My work involves annotating CAZymes across the _B. fragilis_ pangenome and _Bacteroides_ type strains using both dbCAN and Cayman. I compare the tools' predictions with a focus on substrate coverage and specificity for ECM-relevant glycans, assessing which tool more thoroughly identifies host-derived glycan substrates and associated CAZyme families.

Within the _B. fragilis_ pangenome, I also use strain-level source metadata to compare CAZyme annotation profiles between strains isolated from disease and healthy sources. This analysis examines whether ECM-degrading potential differs according to disease association.

Together, these analyses help clarify how annotation tool choice affects the inferred ECM-degrading capacity of a _Bacteroides_ genome or pangenome and inform more consistent interpretation of CAZyme predictions in future gut microbiome studies, including research relevant to inflammatory bowel disease.

</div>
